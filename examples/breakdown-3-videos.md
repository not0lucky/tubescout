All three transcripts are in. Here's the breakdown.

# Verdict

**Two of the three hold up reasonably well; the third is mostly back-of-napkin math dressed as revenue reveals.** Will Ray's video is the most honest of the batch (he shows a partial dashboard and volunteers what's inconsistent), Jake's Starter Story episode contains real tactical detail but classic survivorship framing, and the Niche Pursuits video's headline numbers are largely the creator's own estimates from SimilarWeb traffic × an assumed ad rate — plus its Dec 2024 SEO-display-ad thesis has aged badly. The strongest signal is where all three independently converge: **simple product, distribution over build.**

## Video 1 — Hey Will Ray: "$8K a Month With Just HTML and CSS" (Jul 22, 2026)

**What it actually says:** Best month cleared $15,000. Recurring is "about $8,000 a month across 16 clients" — but the Stripe dashboard he shows reads $6,500, and he says the rest pay "in checks" and other forms. Majority of clients are on $97/month hosting-and-maintenance plans; SEO packages run $500–$3,500/month; one recent deal was $10,000 + $2,500/month for two sites. He started by selling one-page sites at $250 to local contractors. Client acquisition: mine Facebook/Nextdoor local groups and Google Maps for businesses already advertising, then send an AI-assisted **demo site** with the prospect's own logo, phone number, and photos pulled from their Facebook.

**Skeptic pass:**
- *Incentive:* He sells an ebook, one-on-one coaching, and calls (pitch is the last ~15%, plus a Hostinger affiliate link). So the video is a funnel — but a modest one.
- *Verifiability:* This is the rare creator who shows the number that's *lower* than his claim and explains the gap, admits income is lumpy ("anybody telling you this is consistent is lying"), and volunteers bootcamp debt and a criminal record. That pattern is the opposite of typical income-claim inflation. The unverified $1,500/month gap (checks) is plausible but asserted.
- *Survivorship:* Low risk here — $8k/month across 16 small local clients is an unremarkable, widely replicated agency model, not a lottery outcome. The demo-site method is a process anyone can run.
- *Engagement:* 47.7 likes/1k views — top of the normal band, audience clearly found it credible.

**Survives:** the $97/month recurring-hosting model, the demo-site outreach method, "price by the client's existing revenue," "higher-paying clients are easier." **Doesn't survive scrutiny:** nothing is outright suspect, but the $8k figure should be read as "≈$6.5k verified + ≈$1.5k claimed."

## Video 2 — Starter Story: Jake Glacer's "Erly" alarm app, $50K/month (Jul 5, 2026)

**What it actually says:** Alarm app where push-ups (verified by camera analysis) turn off the alarm. Launched end of 2025; "$50,000 a month in 4 months"; 200,000+ downloads in 2026, ~50,000 downloads and "a little over $50,000" in the last 28 days. Pricing: free trial, then $29.99/year or $9.99/month. Build timeline: 19 days from first commit to App Store (without the alarm), alarm feature 19 days later — enabled by iOS 26's new AlarmKit API. Growth: influencer deals of 2–4 videos/month with **view guarantees at $2–3 CPM**, targeting day-in-the-life creators where the app slots in natively; "put in 5K and you get out 10"; returns follow a power law and "you're going to lose money on individual creators." Stack: Firebase, Superwall, OpenAI API, Mixpanel, Claude Code, plus influencer-ops tooling (Instantly for mass outreach, Google Sheets, etc.).

**Skeptic pass:**
- *Incentive:* Two layers. Starter Story's whole channel is a funnel to its free "iOS App Ideas" database (email capture) — they only ever feature winners. Jake gets free distribution for Erly and his personal brand. Neither incentive requires the numbers to be false, but neither party would air a failure.
- *Survivorship:* Heavy. Jake himself says "I had some other businesses in the past, nothing really worked." You're seeing attempt N after N−1 failures, and Starter Story's format filters out every founder for whom $2–3 CPM influencer seeding didn't convert.
- *Verifiability:* Revenue screenshots were shown to the host on camera, which beats pure assertion but isn't independently checkable. Also note **$50k is gross App Store revenue** — Apple takes 15–30%, and influencer spend comes out of the rest, so profit is meaningfully lower than the headline. The timing claim (AlarmKit made real alarm apps possible for the first time) is a genuine, checkable structural fact and the most load-bearing part of the story.
- *Freshness:* Very fresh, but that's also the catch — the AlarmKit window is a first-mover advantage that closes as clones flood in.

**Survives:** the influencer playbook (view-guaranteed deals, $2–3 CPM, native-fit creators, track spikes not attribution), the ship-in-19-days scoping discipline, "inherently viral or add a novel aspect until it is." **Doesn't survive:** treating the outcome as reproducible — the result rode a one-time API window plus power-law luck on a few multi-million-view videos, which Jake half-admits.

## Video 3 — Niche Pursuits: "BORING Website Makes $35,000/Month" (Dec 23, 2024)

**What it actually says:** Four sites. (1) unitconverters.net: 4.5M visits/month per SimilarWeb, 1.55 pages/visit, and **he plugs an assumed $5 RPM into a calculator himself** to get ">$35,000/month." (2) A spreadsheet-template site ("chartsheets"): templates at ~$10–$170, "the founder has revealed" up to $40,000/month. (3) typingtest.com: 3.6M visits/month, 8.05 pages/visit, again a self-computed estimate at an assumed $5 RPM (the caption garbles the figure; his own math implies roughly $140k+/month, and he later says "$100,000 per month" rhetorically). (4) smallpdf.com: 57M monthly organic visits, revenue "$17.5 million a year according to Google AI Overviews" or $12.5M per GrowJo.

**Skeptic pass:**
- *Incentive:* Newsletter signups, his Link Whisper WordPress plugin, and an explicit mid-video subscribe plea tied to a 50k-subscriber goal. The video exists to demonstrate that SEO niches work — which is what his whole business sells.
- *Verifiability:* Weakest of the three by far. Three of the four revenue figures are the creator's own traffic-times-assumed-RPM arithmetic or a citation of *Google AI Overviews* as a revenue source. Only the template-site figure traces to a founder statement, and even that is "up to $40k," unshown.
- *Freshness:* This is the killer. Published Dec 2024, and the entire model for sites 1 and 3 — informational utility queries monetized by display ads — is precisely what Google's AI Overviews and built-in SERP tools (Google has answered "inches to feet" directly in search results for years) have been eroding since. In mid-2026, "rank for unit conversions and collect $5 RPMs" is advice from a platform era that's ending. SmallPDF (freemium product, not ads) is the only one of the four whose model still looks durable.

**Survives:** the general observation that boring utility niches monetize, and the freemium-tool model (SmallPDF). **Doesn't survive:** every headline dollar figure except arguably the template site, and the SEO-display-ads playbook as a thing to start in 2026.

## Cross-reference: where independent creators agree

These three have different incentives (coaching funnel, media channel, SEO-tools funnel), which makes their agreement the real intelligence:

1. **Simple beats complex, explicitly.** Will: "a plumber does not need a web app." Jake: "solve a painful problem in a simple way… limit your scope." Niche Pursuits: entire thesis. Three-for-three.
2. **Distribution is the business; the build is a commodity.** Will's demo-site outreach, Jake's "building doesn't really matter so much, what matters is marketing," Niche Pursuits' traffic-first framing. Three-for-three, and Jake and Will both attribute the build commoditization to AI specifically.
3. **Show, don't pitch.** Will's demo site with the prospect's own logo and Jake's native-fit influencer video are the same move: put the finished thing in front of the buyer so they don't have to imagine it.
4. **Where they diverge:** the *channel*. Local-lead SEO (Will) and short-form influencer seeding (Jake) are both alive in 2026; informational SEO for display ads (Niche Pursuits) is the one channel of the three that platform changes have visibly undercut.

## What this means for you

You're already running most of Will Ray's playbook — demo sites for niches (dental-site, solterra, marsh-and-vale, meridian on the VPS) and niche research tooling. The gap his video exposes isn't build capability, it's the **$97/month recurring layer**: he converts one-off builds into 16 small retainers, and that's what makes the model survivable through lumpy months. If any of your demo verticals get a live client, the hosting-plus-maintenance retainer is the piece worth copying, and his outreach source (business owners self-identifying in local Facebook/Nextdoor comment threads) is a concrete, free lead list.

Jake's video is relevant to your iOS pipeline: the transferable parts are the 19-day scope cap, the "is the demo inherently viral in a 10-second clip" test applied *before* building, and view-guaranteed micro-influencer deals at $2–3 CPM as a bounded-risk marketing experiment. Skip the Niche Pursuits model — you'd be entering informational SEO exactly as its monetization collapses; the one durable idea in it (freemium utility tool à la SmallPDF) is better executed as a product, not an ad-supported content site.
